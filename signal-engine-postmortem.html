Diagnostic Review · BTC Signal Engine

Signal Engine Post-Mortem

Why the calls feel late, why the performance numbers can't be trusted, and the twelve defects behind both — ordered by what to fix first.

Verdict

The signals aren't late. The position construction is. The engine emits a directional call on 18–65% of all bars depending on regime — it is hyperactive, not hesitant. What arrives late is the fill: maxPositionPct is applied as a fraction of remaining cash, so each buy deploys 25% of what's left, then 25% of the rest, and so on. You approach full exposure asymptotically and never reach it, and you never fully exit. Your average fill lands 0.78% above the price of the signal that triggered you on entries, and 0.48% below it on exits.

Underneath that sits a second, larger problem: the instrumentation that would have told you this is dead. Hold signals are never resolved, so opportunity-cost accounting, the λ penalty, and a third of the champion–challenger sample have been silently inert this whole time. Combined with zero fee modelling and a leaking optimizer split, the reported performance figures do not measure what they claim to.

Method & what this rests on

This environment has no DATABASE_URL and no network egress to the exchanges, so I could not read your actual signal history or replay real candles. Every defect below is a fact about the code and holds regardless. Every number comes from running your real computeAllMetrics and generateSignal over synthetic series with mathematically known turning points, at the same fixed 336-bar window production uses — they are directionally reliable and reproduce the mechanism, but they are illustrations, not measurements of your live P&L. Re-run the harness against real history before trusting any specific figure.

Two hypotheses I started with turned out to be wrong, and I've dropped them: the 2-of-2 confirmation buffer adds only 0–1 bars of delay, and the minConfidence gate is loose rather than restrictive (65.8% of bars clear it; raw scores reach 1.38).

P0 · Correctness bugs that void your metrics

P0-1

Hold signals are never resolved, so the entire opportunity-cost system is inert

server/db.ts:185–191 · server/heartbeatHandler.ts:475–487

getPendingSignals() filters signal != 'hold'. Holds therefore keep outcome = 'pending' permanently and updateSignalOutcome is never called on them. This makes the hold_correct / hold_missed branch in runSignalValidation unreachable dead code, pins holdRegret, holdMissed and holdCorrect in the validation log at zero forever, and means OPPORTUNITY_COST_LAMBDA has no effect in production at all — it exists only inside the backtest. It also starves champion–challenger: getResolvedSignalsSince selects outcome != 'pending', so the paired t-test has never seen a single hold from any variant.

Fix

Drop the != 'hold' predicate. The heartbeat's validator already classifies holds correctly; the manual path in routers.ts:155–200 does not, so give it the same hold branch or have both call one shared resolver. Expect win-rate and reward figures to move materially once holds start counting.

P0-2

Scheduler state lives in module memory and resets on every cold start

server/heartbeatHandler.ts:32–33, 170

Same class of bug as the confirmation buffer we just fixed, and three more instances survive. lastSignalRunTs resets to 0, which makes signalThrottled evaluate false on every cold invocation — so signal generation runs on every single heartbeat regardless of the heartbeatScheduleMinutes you configured. That is the direct explanation for the signal volume you saw over 12 hours. lastOptimizeHour and lastWeeklyReportDay reset the same way, so walk-forward optimization can re-run on every heartbeat during hour 0 and the weekly report can be written repeatedly all Sunday — churning strategy versions and duplicating weekly_performance rows.

Fix

Derive all three from the database; no schema change needed. lastSignalRunTs → MAX(ts) over champion signals. lastOptimizeHour → createdAt of the newest [Auto] strategy version. lastWeeklyReportDay → MAX(weekEnd) in weekly_performance.

P0-3

No fee, spread or slippage anywhere — and the objective function rewards overtrading

walkForwardOptimizer.ts:102–113 · heartbeatHandler.ts:369–401 · routers.ts:656–678

All three execution paths fill instantly at candle.close at zero cost. On its own that just inflates returns. The real damage is the interaction: the optimizer maximises totalReturn − λ × holdRegret, so holding is penalised while trading is free. The search is structurally biased toward the most hyperactive parameter sets, and the "aggressive" challenger — which trades more by construction — carries a systematically overstated edge into the promotion t-test.

Fix

Add a single FEE_BPS constant (≈13 bps/side ≈ Kraken taker 0.26% round trip) and apply it in all three paths plus historicalSignalReplay. This is the highest-leverage single line in the codebase: it re-scores every parameter set the optimizer has ever chosen.

P0-4

Confirmation can be satisfied by two signals hours apart

server/heartbeatHandler.ts:347–360

My DB-backed rewrite filters holds out before taking the last two champion signals, which preserves the original in-memory semantics — but with no time bound. A BUY, twenty hours of HOLD, then another BUY now counts as confirmation. That is a stale confirmation acting on a market state that no longer exists.

Fix

Require both confirming signals to fall within a bounded window — roughly 2.5 × heartbeatScheduleMinutes — and otherwise treat the run as broken and restart it.

P1 · Strategy logic defects

P1-1

Position sizing is a fraction of what's left, not a target allocation

heartbeatHandler.ts:370–397 · routers.ts:656–678 · walkForwardOptimizer.ts:104–112

This is the mechanism behind your "too late" instinct. tradeUsd = cash × maxPositionPct spends 25% of remaining cash, so consecutive buys deploy 25%, 18.75%, 14.1%… — 57.8% invested after three signals, 76.3% after five, never 100%. Exits decay identically, so you hold through most of a decline. Because directional episodes average ~7.7 consecutive signals, you spend the whole move averaging in at progressively worse prices. Measured against the price at the first signal of each episode: entries fill 0.78% higher, exits 0.48% lower. Under full target sizing the same signal stream produced the same return in 6 trades instead of 398.

Fix

Switch to target exposure: compute a desired allocation (scaled by confidence if you want conviction sizing), then trade only the difference between current and target. A buy moves you toward the target in one decisive fill; a sell moves you toward zero and actually gets you out. This alone should change the felt behaviour more than any indicator change.

P1-2

The EMA component votes on state, not on the crossover event

server/engine/signalGenerator.ts:120–139

The branch is if (diff > 0) → buy, which is true for the entire duration of an uptrend rather than at the moment of the cross. Over the test window there were 6 actual EMA12/26 crossings but 720 directional votes, at a mean age of 58 bars after the cross. The component is a persistent directional bias wearing the name "crossover". It contributes regime information and zero timing information — yet it is weighted as if it were an entry trigger.

Fix

Split the two jobs. Keep EMA state as a regime filter that gates which direction is permitted, and add a genuine trigger — the crossing event itself, or a MACD-histogram zero-cross — as the thing that actually times entry. Only the trigger should carry entry weight.

P1-3

"Z-Score Trend" is mean-reversion logic filed under trend-following, so Hurst amplifies it when it's most wrong

signalGenerator.ts:141–159 and :199

The direction is z > 0 ? "sell" : "buy" — it fades the move, which is mean-reverting behaviour. But the name sits in the trendFollowing set, so it receives trendMult and gets amplified exactly when Hurst says price is trending. In the run, 100% of its directional votes were cast under amplification, and 77% of them were wrong on the next 24 hours. The regime-weighting machinery is working correctly; it is being handed a mislabelled input.

Fix

Move "Z-Score Trend" into the meanReverting set so Hurst damps it in trends — a one-line change. If you intended it as genuinely trend-following, invert its direction instead. Do one or the other; today it is the worst of both.

P1-4

Fix

Gate them on regime rather than merely scaling them: let RSI and Bollinger Bands vote only when ADX < 25 or Hurst < 0.45. In a confirmed trend they should be silent, not quiet.

P2 · Evaluation and testing defects

P2-1

The walk-forward split leaks — the test set contains the training set

walkForwardOptimizer.ts:230–243

trainData = candles.slice(0, splitIdx) but testData = candles — the full array, training window included. The "validation" pass re-scores candidates on data they were selected on, so backtestReturnPct and friends are in-sample figures presented as out-of-sample. With only 16 random ±20% jitter variations this is also a thin search, as the README already concedes.

Fix

testData = candles.slice(splitIdx). Expect the reported backtest numbers to drop — that drop is the overfit you have been carrying.

P2-2

The optimizer backtests on a growing window; production runs on a fixed one

walkForwardOptimizer.ts:86 vs shared/tradingTypes.ts:20–24

The backtest feeds candles.slice(0, i + 1) — an ever-growing history — while production always passes exactly getCandleLimit(interval) bars (336 at 1h). Hurst, z-score, Bollinger and RSI all depend on window length, so parameters are fitted under indicator conditions that never occur live.

Fix

Slice a fixed trailing window in the backtest loop to match getCandleLimit exactly.

P2-3

Outcome labelling is a single point sample, and the simulator tests assert against themselves

heartbeatHandler.ts:472–487 · server/simulator.test.ts

A signal is scored win/loss purely on price at the horizon, ignoring the path — a trade that ran 5% against you before closing 0.1% ahead is a "win". There is no stop, no target, no MFE/MAE. Separately, the "Simulator Trade Execution Logic" suite never imports executeSimulatorTrade; it re-implements the arithmetic inside each test and asserts against its own copy. Those tests would still pass if the simulator were deleted, which is why P1-1 shipped undetected.

Fix

Record max favourable and max adverse excursion over the horizon and label against a stop/target pair. Rewrite the simulator suite to call the real exported function, including the isRunning gate and insufficient-balance paths.

Suggested order of work

Tranche 1 Make the numbers mean something

Nothing else can be evaluated until these land, because today's metrics can't distinguish a good change from a bad one.

  1. P0-1 — resolve holds; unblocks λ, hold regret, and the challenger sample.
  2. P0-3 — add FEE_BPS to all execution paths.
  3. P2-1 — fix the leaking train/test split.
  4. P0-2 — move scheduler state into the database; stops the runaway signal cadence and version churn.

Tranche 2 Fix the felt problem

This is what addresses "the calls come too late" directly, and it is mostly independent of the indicator work.

  1. P1-1 — target-exposure sizing instead of fraction-of-remaining.
  2. P0-4 — time-bound the confirmation window.
  3. P2-3 — real tests around the simulator before changing it further.

Tranche 3 Repair the signal itself

Do this last and re-measure after each step — with Tranche 1 in place you will finally be able to tell whether a change helped.

  1. P1-3 — reclassify Z-Score Trend (one line, clear win).
  2. P1-4 — regime-gate RSI and Bollinger Bands.
  3. P1-2 — separate regime filter from entry trigger.
  4. P2-2 — align backtest window with production.

Beyond Enhancements worth considering after the above

  • Volatility-scaled sizing. Size by ATR or realised vol so a fixed dollar risk survives regime shifts, rather than a fixed fraction of equity.
  • An explicit exit policy. Today exits happen only when the composite flips. A trailing stop or time stop would cap the tail losses the current win/loss labelling cannot even see.
  • Retire the modifier stack until it is validated out-of-sample. The replay script carries its own overfitting caveat, and the README judges the IBIT proxy "directionally ambiguous… does not add signal" — yet it ships in the engine at ×1.20.
  • Recalibrate λ once holds resolve. The 0.3 default is self-documented as indistinguishable across [0, 1.5] — but that test ran while hold accounting was dead in production, so it deserves a fresh look.
